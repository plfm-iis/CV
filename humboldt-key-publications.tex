\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Five Key Publications}\par
{\normalsize Yu-Fang Chen}\par
{\small Humboldt Research Award nomination | October 2026}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.3em}
These five publications trace a sustained research programme: foundational algorithms for automata, their development into competitive string-solving technology, and their application to the automated verification of quantum computations. The selection combines established long-term influence with recent advances opening new research directions.\par
\section{1. When Simulation Meets Antichains (TACAS 2010)}
\textbf{Authors:} Parosh Aziz Abdulla, Yu-Fang Chen, Lukáš Holík, Richard Mayr, Tomáš Vojnar.\\
\textbf{Reference:} TACAS 2010, pp. 158--174. \textbf{Recognition:} Best Paper Award.

This paper brought together simulation relations and antichain search to make language-inclusion checking for nondeterministic automata substantially more effective. Its contribution has endured well beyond the original experiments. Simulation-enhanced antichain methods were implemented in the VATA automata library, independently reimplemented and benchmarked in later research, and extended to refinement checking in the PAT model checker. Subsequent research also revisited the underlying algorithms through the theory of complete abstractions for language inclusion. More than a decade later, VATA and its inclusion procedures continued to appear as experimental reference points in new automata-library comparisons. The award-winning result thus exemplifies a foundational idea that has been implemented, tested, and extended by independent research teams.

\section{2. Word Equations in Synergy with Regular Constraints (FM 2023)}
\textbf{Authors:} František Blahoudek, Yu-Fang Chen, David Chocholatý, Vojtěch Havlena, Lukáš Holík, Ondřej Lengál, Juraj Síč.\\
\textbf{Reference:} FM 2023, pp. 403--423. \textbf{Recognition:} Best Paper Award.

This Best Paper Award-winning work introduced a stabilization-based approach that combines word equations with regular-language constraints, allowing the two forms of reasoning to reinforce rather than obstruct each other. Its significance is demonstrated by a direct progression from algorithmic research to practical solver development: the original Noodler prototype evolved into Z3-Noodler, an automata-based string solver integrated with Z3. Subsequent work extended this approach with additional decision procedures, model generation, and support for further classes of string constraints. Importantly, the research stimulated independent development beyond the original collaboration: a team at Beihang University extended and optimized Z3-Noodler to develop Z3-Noodler-Mocha. Together, Z3-Noodler and Z3-Noodler-Mocha won all five scoring categories of the QF\_Strings Single Query Track at SMT-COMP for three consecutive years (2024--2026): Z3-Noodler in 2024 and 2026, and Z3-Noodler-Mocha in 2025. This combination of sustained solver development, independent extensions, and international competition results demonstrates the practical impact of the underlying research programme.

\section{3. Solving String Constraints with Lengths by Stabilization (OOPSLA 2023)}
\textbf{Authors:} Yu-Fang Chen, David Chocholatý, Vojtěch Havlena, Lukáš Holík, Ondřej Lengál, Juraj Síč.\\
\textbf{Reference:} Proceedings of the ACM on Programming Languages 7 (OOPSLA2), pp. 2112--2141, 2023. \textbf{Recognition:} Distinguished Paper Award.

This paper addressed a central obstacle in string solving: combining reasoning about word equations and regular constraints with arithmetic properties of string lengths. Its length-aware stabilization, alignment, and splitting mechanisms made this interaction amenable to an automata-based solving architecture. Its impact is visible in identifiable technical extensions, rather than merely in citations: subsequent procedures for string--integer conversion reused length-aware noodlification, research on position constraints built on variable-isolation techniques, and later work on transducers extended the interaction with length reasoning. The resulting Z3-Noodler solver has also been evaluated by independent research groups in general string-solving and cloud-policy benchmarks. Together with the OOPSLA Distinguished Paper Award, these developments demonstrate how a fundamental advance in reasoning about lengths became a reusable component of subsequent solver research.

\section{4. An Automata-Based Framework for Verification and Bug Hunting in Quantum Circuits (PLDI 2023)}
\textbf{Authors:} Yu-Fang Chen, Kai-Min Chung, Ondřej Lengál, Jyun-Ao Lin, Wei-Lun Tsai, Di-De Yen.\\
\textbf{Reference:} Proceedings of the ACM on Programming Languages 7 (PLDI), pp. 1218--1243, 2023. \textbf{Recognition:} Distinguished Paper Award; \emph{Communications of the ACM} Research Highlights (2025).

This paper established a new connection between automata theory and quantum-circuit verification. By representing entire sets of quantum states symbolically and applying gates directly to these representations, it made automated verification and bug finding possible without explicitly enumerating state vectors. The AutoQ framework established a sustained research programme: subsequent work developed more compact automata, symbolic specifications, and verification techniques for quantum programs involving measurements and classical control. Importantly, AutoQ has also been used outside the original collaboration: researchers at DLR, the University of Bremen, and DFKI executed it as an experimental comparator in their work on quantum-circuit fault identification. The paper received a PLDI Distinguished Paper Award and was selected for the \emph{Communications of the ACM} Research Highlights in 2025. Chen's invited keynote at Highlights 2025 on automata-based quantum program verification further demonstrates the international recognition of the research direction initiated by this work.

\section{5. Verifying Quantum Circuits with Level-Synchronized Tree Automata (POPL 2025)}
\textbf{Authors:} Parosh Aziz Abdulla, Yo-Ga Chen, Yu-Fang Chen, Lukáš Holík, Ondřej Lengál, Jyun-Ao Lin, Fang-Yi Lo, Wei-Lun Tsai.\\
\textbf{Reference:} Proceedings of the ACM on Programming Languages 9 (POPL), pp. 923--953, 2025.

This paper introduced level-synchronized tree automata (LSTAs), a new symbolic model that coordinates choices across tree levels, allowing relationships between subtrees to be represented compactly while preserving the algorithmic properties needed for verification. For supported quantum-gate operations, it replaced the exponential worst-case growth of the earlier representation with at most quadratic growth. More importantly, the new formalism has already enabled concrete developments: a practical specification language compiles properties into LSTAs, verification of repeat-until-success protocols uses synchronized choices to preserve input--output relations, and parameterized quantum verification develops the synchronization principle further through weighted automata. Beyond these collaborative advances, an independent Canadian--Belgian research team has begun investigating LSTA decision complexity and representation growth in a 2026 preprint. These developments illustrate how a new automata model has opened both additional verification capabilities and new theoretical research questions.
